Instead of copying what other coaches say their role is, I ask a simpler question: **what is coaching fundamentally for?** When you reason from the ground up, the scope of practice becomes much clearer.

## 1. Coaching is about building forward capacity

Coaching is not primarily about analysing the past. It is about building the capacity to move forward — helping someone strengthen the skills and structures that allow them to live more intentionally.

That includes things like:

- Decision-making
- Self-awareness
- Communication
- Emotional regulation
- Relational discernment
- Structure and follow-through
- Alignment between values and behaviour

A coach works in the domain of growth and expansion. The core question is not *why did this happen to you?* It is *what capacity would help you move forward from here?*

## 2. Coaching works with the present and the future

The past is not ignored in coaching, but it is not the primary object of work.

If someone mentions a childhood experience, a relationship history, or a pattern that formed earlier in life, a coach may acknowledge it and explore how it influences present behaviour. The goal is not to process or treat the past itself.

The questions a coach asks tend to sound like:

- How does that pattern show up in your life today?
- What would you like to do differently going forward?
- What structures would support the life you want to build?

Coaching is future-oriented. The work is about authorship, not excavation.

## 3. Coaching does not diagnose or treat pathology

A life coach does not diagnose mental health conditions and does not treat psychological disorders. Diagnosis belongs to licensed clinicians. That means a coach does not determine whether someone has conditions such as ADHD, autism, depression, or trauma-related disorders. If those topics arise, a coach may encourage the client to seek evaluation from qualified professionals.

This is not a limitation of coaching. It is a boundary that protects both the client and the integrity of the work. Coaching focuses on capacity and behaviour, not clinical treatment.

## 4. Coaching respects when therapeutic support is needed

Sometimes the right support for a person is therapy rather than coaching. Where someone is experiencing acute psychological distress, that situation requires clinical care, and an ethical coach recognises it and recommends therapeutic support.

This is not a failure of coaching. It is responsible practice. Different professions exist because different forms of support require different training.

## 5. Coaching works with patterns, not pathologies

Human beings are pattern-forming creatures. We develop habits of thinking, reacting, relating, and deciding, and many of those patterns are adaptive responses to real life experiences.

Coaching can explore those patterns directly — communication habits in relationships, avoidance or procrastination, overcommitment and people-pleasing, difficulty setting boundaries, decision paralysis, cycles of motivation and burnout.

The focus is not on labelling a disorder. It is on helping someone see their patterns clearly and build new ones that support the life they want.

## 6. Coaching is a partnership in self-authorship

At its core, coaching is collaborative. The client brings their life, their goals, and their questions. The coach brings structure, reflection, and accountability.

The coach does not prescribe an identity or diagnose a problem to be fixed. The work centres on authorship: clarifying what matters, understanding current patterns, and building the structures needed to move forward.

## Why the principles matter

Without clear principles, the boundaries of coaching become blurry. Some coaches drift into therapy without the training to do it safely. Others retreat so far from emotional depth that coaching becomes shallow productivity advice.

Life coaching exists to help people build the capacity to live more intentionally, make better decisions, and align their actions with the life they want to create. It is forward-looking work, and practised within its proper scope, it is a genuinely useful form of support.

This is the foundation I operate from. Coaching is not therapy. It is not diagnosis. It is not treatment. It is a structured partnership designed to help someone author their own life.
