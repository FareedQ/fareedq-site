## How to read this page

These terms are not additional pillars and they are not diagnoses. They are working descriptions that help identify the moment when something useful — an ambition, a plan, a desire — has shifted into obligation, control, rigidity, or avoidance. They are written to be recognised in yourself, not diagnosed in others.

Most of them are not from a textbook. Some are my own language, developed from patterns that kept appearing in conversations. They are held loosely, and they are revised as I understand them better.

## Obligation-triggered desire collapse

A pattern in which an activity or aspiration that once felt genuinely wanted begins to feel primarily like an expectation, a duty, or a test. As the obligation grows, spontaneous desire diminishes — sometimes to nothing.

This does not establish a single cause, and it does not mean that every loss of motivation is this. Sometimes you are simply tired, or bored, or no longer interested, and those are real answers too. But it is a useful inquiry: has the scale, the timing, the external pressure, the self-judgment, or the loss of choice changed your relationship to this thing?

The responses tend to be practical rather than psychological. Restore genuine options. Reduce the demand. Distinguish a necessary commitment from the desire that originally made it meaningful. Sometimes the honest conclusion is that the desire was real and the obligation has simply grown too large for it to survive, and the work is to make the obligation smaller rather than to manufacture motivation.

## Clean desire and anticipatory charge

**Clean desire** is wanting that is relatively open, present-oriented, and not organised around securing a particular result. It is wanting that could survive a change in circumstances without collapsing into anxiety.

**Anticipatory charge** is the pressure that accumulates around possibility — through repeated rehearsal, urgency, imagined outcomes, or self-worth becoming tied to something that has not happened yet. It feels like desire. It is often much more intense than desire, and it usually collapses the moment obligation appears.

Neither term labels strong feeling as bad. Enthusiasm and anticipation can be healthy and enjoyable. The practical distinction is whether the wanting still permits curiosity, flexibility, and a degree of non-attachment to the outcome — or whether it has become a demand for certainty. One is sustainable and the other spends itself.

## Containment

Containment is the ability to experience powerful emotion or uncertainty without being required to suppress it or to act immediately in order to resolve it. It is not suppression; suppression is what happens when the feeling has nowhere to go. Containment is the capacity to let something be felt while you decide what, if anything, to do.

It develops the way most capacity develops: in increments, with recovery in between. A conversation that is uncomfortable and does not have to be won. A decision that can stay open for a week. A desire that can be felt without being announced.

## The maintenance trap and over-optimisation

A **maintenance trap** occurs when the work of sustaining your obligations crowds out expansion and enjoyment. Everything is necessary; nothing is chosen. The week functions, and there is no room left in it for anything that is not a requirement.

**Over-optimisation** is the related move in which recovery and pleasure are converted into further performance requirements: sleep tracked and optimised, rest scheduled and measured, hobbies with a return on investment. What was meant to restore capacity becomes one more thing to do well.

The two overlap, and they often form a loop. Stress tightens the schedule, which creates more obligation, which reduces the space for participation and makes the stress feel more threatening.

## Chronic activation

Chronic Yellow names persistent activation without meaningful recovery or choice — the ambient condition described on the [regulation page](/framework/regulation/). It is worth naming separately because it is easy to mistake for a personality, or for evidence that you are the kind of person who needs a lot of structure, or that something is fundamentally wrong with you.

## The point of noticing

Recognition is meant to invite a proportionate adjustment, not to become a new standard for self-criticism. If you find yourself reading this page and concluding that you are failing at all five patterns at once, that response is itself one of the patterns — the conversion of an insight into an obligation.

The useful question is always local: what is one thing here that could be made slightly smaller, slightly more chosen, or slightly better supported this week?
