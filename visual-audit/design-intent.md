# FareedQ.com — Visual Design Intent

## Purpose

This document describes the intended visual identity, design principles, meaningful graphic elements, and experience goals of FareedQ.com.

It provides context to human designers and AI visual auditors.

It is not a declaration that every current design decision is successful.

**An intentional design choice may still be visually ineffective and should be criticized when appropriate.**

The purpose of this document is to help an auditor distinguish between accidental defects, deliberate design decisions, and opportunities for refinement.

## 1. Website Identity

FareedQ.com is a personal coaching website built around the philosophy of Authentic Manifestation.

The website introduces the coach, explains the philosophy, presents a small coaching pilot, and invites expressions of interest.

It is not intended to resemble a high-volume coaching funnel, generic wellness brand, corporate consultancy, or technology startup.

The desired visual identity is:

- Editorial and intellectually engaging.
- Warm, personal, and approachable.
- Grounded rather than aspirational or performative.
- Distinctive without being eccentric for its own sake.
- Professional without feeling institutional.
- Calm without becoming visually lifeless.
- Expressive without becoming cluttered.

The overall aesthetic can be understood as **refined editorial design with contemporary human warmth**.

The website should feel like an invitation to engage with a thoughtful person and a body of ideas.

## 2. Coaching Philosophy and Its Visual Implications

The coaching philosophy is Authentic Manifestation.

Its three primary pillars are:

1. Nervous System Regulation.
2. Minimal Viable Life.
3. Participation.

Participation includes the supporting concepts of Authorship and Responsible Non-Ownership.

These concepts should not be represented as a competitive ranking, fixed progression, or hierarchy of personal worth.

The website's visual language should support the philosophy's emphasis on participation, agency, sustainable living, and awareness of capacity.

Avoid visual metaphors suggesting that a person must become optimized, perfected, or fully regulated before participating in life.

Graphics should explain relationships and concepts rather than merely decorate the page.

## 3. Typography

The existing typography system pairs:

**Archivo:** structural and display typography, navigation, labels, headings, and selected emphasis.

**Georgia:** editorial reading, reflective passages, and longer explanatory text.

This contrast is intentional.

Archivo provides clarity and structure.

Georgia provides a more literary and human reading experience.

The pairing should be preserved unless there is a compelling reason to change it.

Small uppercase section labels are intentional navigational devices.

However, the auditor should still assess whether typography is readable, proportionate, well-spaced, and visually balanced.

Intentional typography is not exempt from criticism.

## 4. Colour

The established palette includes:

- Deep navy.
- White.
- Warm off-white and paper tones.
- Restrained rust accents.

The palette is intended to feel composed, warm, and credible.

The website should not default to the pastel gradients, soft green branding, or decorative botanical motifs commonly associated with generic wellness websites.

Colour should serve composition, hierarchy, emotional tone, and meaning.

The use of a restrained palette does not mean every section must look similar.

Distinct background tones are encouraged where they create meaningful page rhythm.

Colour may be used more expressively in conceptual diagrams, particularly the nervous-system zones.

Preserve accessibility and readable contrast.

## 5. Layout and Composition

The website uses an editorial layout system featuring:

- Constrained reading widths.
- Generous whitespace.
- Asymmetrical compositions.
- Split sections with headings and content in separate columns.
- Hairline dividers.
- Restrained callouts.
- Large, confident headings.

These elements are intentional.

The site generally prefers typographic hierarchy and spacing over heavy cards, shadows, and boxed components.

However, the auditor must distinguish between effective whitespace and accidental emptiness.

Repeated split sections may become monotonous.

A deliberately asymmetrical layout may still be poorly balanced.

A restrained section may still lack visual interest.

The goal is compositional quality, not rigid preservation of every existing layout.

## 6. Meaningful Graphic Motifs

### The three-pillar growing-rule motif

The website uses a graphic motif involving progressively extended horizontal rules associated with the three-pillar framework.

This is an intentional conceptual motif.

It should not automatically be interpreted as a progress indicator, loading bar, or broken step component.

However, the auditor may assess whether the motif successfully communicates its purpose or creates confusion.

### Nervous-system zones

The Green, Yellow, and Red zones represent different experiences of nervous-system state and capacity.

These are conceptual categories, not a moral hierarchy.

Green should not be presented as a superior type of person, and Red should not be represented as personal failure.

A visual explanation should support understanding without implying that states always occur in a fixed sequence.

### Three-pillar framework

The three pillars should be presented as related and mutually supporting concepts.

They should not be visually ranked by importance unless the underlying content explicitly establishes such a distinction.

## 7. Photography

Authentic photography is preferred over generic stock photography.

The existing homepage portrait is a significant part of the site's identity.

Its colourful clothing and human presence provide warmth and personality against the restrained visual system.

Photography should communicate an actual person in an actual environment.

Avoid stereotypical wellness imagery, staged meditation poses, anonymous smiling professionals, and generic nature photography used without conceptual purpose.

Additional photography may be appropriate, but it should be purposeful rather than inserted to satisfy an image quota.

## 8. Interaction and Motion

Interactions should support clarity, orientation, and engagement.

Motion should be purposeful, accessible, performant, and coherent with the site's visual language.

The website should not use animation simply to appear modern.

Avoid excessive scroll effects, distracting transitions, or interactions that interfere with reading.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

Prefer straightforward implementations over unnecessary frameworks.

## 9. Coaching Invitations and Pricing

The website presents a small coaching pilot.

Its invitations should feel clear, respectful, and low-pressure.

The visual language should not rely on:

- Artificial urgency.
- Countdown timers.
- Aggressive scarcity messaging.
- Repeated conversion interruptions.
- Manipulative visual hierarchy.

Pricing should be legible and easy to compare.

Primary actions should be discoverable without dominating every section.

The objective is to help visitors make an informed decision about whether coaching is appropriate for them.

## 10. Responsive Design

Mobile should be treated as a deliberately composed experience, not simply a compressed desktop layout.

The existing mobile homepage uses a photo-first hero arrangement.

This is intentional because it introduces the person before the coaching proposition.

However, the auditor should still evaluate whether the image, headline, introductory copy, and CTA fit together effectively.

Responsive layouts should preserve readability, balance, and useful visual hierarchy.

A technically responsive page may still be poorly composed.

## 11. Accessibility and Performance

Visual quality includes usability.

The design should preserve:

- Readable typography.
- Sufficient colour contrast.
- Visible keyboard focus.
- Appropriate interactive targets.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Responsive layouts without horizontal overflow.
- Reasonable loading performance.
- Clear error and validation states.

Accessibility and performance problems should not be dismissed as intentional aesthetic choices.

## 12. How Auditors Should Use This Document

Treat these rules as context, not unquestionable instructions.

For each potential finding, distinguish between:

**Implementation defect:** Something is broken, inconsistent, misaligned, inaccessible, or visibly unintended.

**Intentional but ineffective design:** The implementation reflects a deliberate choice, but the result is visually confusing, monotonous, unbalanced, or difficult to use.

**Creative opportunity:** A subjective improvement that may make the site more engaging or distinctive.

**Intentional and effective design:** An unusual or distinctive element that successfully serves its purpose.

Do not recommend changing an element merely because it is unconventional.

Do not reject criticism merely because the element is intentional.

When possible, distinguish observations from interpretations.

For example:

Observation: Three horizontal rules have progressively increasing lengths.

Interpretation: They may resemble a progress indicator.

Project context: The rules are an intentional three-pillar motif.

Assessment: Determine whether the motif communicates effectively or needs clarification.

**The ultimate objective is visual quality, conceptual fidelity, and a coherent human experience.**
